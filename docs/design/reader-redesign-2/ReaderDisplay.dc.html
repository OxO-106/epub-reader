Chapter XXIII

The road out of town ran low between the hedges, and by the second milestone the morning had settled into that pale, considering light which makes every field look newly ploughed.

She had meant to think of nothing in particular. It was a habit she had tried, as one tries a borrowed coat, and found that it hung badly on her.

At the bridge a carter had stopped to let his horse drink, and he lifted his hat to her with the slow courtesy of a man who has nowhere he must be by noon.
