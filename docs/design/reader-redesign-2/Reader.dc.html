Chapter XXIII

The road out of town ran low between the hedges, and by the second milestone the morning had settled into that pale, considering light which makes every field look newly ploughed.

She had meant to think of nothing in particular. It was a habit she had tried, as one tries a borrowed coat, and found that it hung badly on her; for the mind, once it has been taught to ask, keeps asking long after the questions have been answered.

At the bridge a carter had stopped to let his horse drink, and he lifted his hat to her with the slow courtesy of a man who has nowhere he must be by noon. She returned it, and walked on, and was a little ashamed to find that she envied him.

There is a kind of patience that belongs only to people who expect very little, and another kind, rarer and more costly, that belongs to those who expect a great deal and have resolved to wait for it. She did not yet know which kind was hers.

The church clock struck the half hour behind her, and the sound came over the water thin and clear, as though it had been rinsed.
