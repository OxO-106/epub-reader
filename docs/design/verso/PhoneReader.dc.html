Middlemarch
Old and Young

Dorothea came in at a quarter to five, with the colour of the lane still in her face and a book under her arm that she had not opened. She kissed her uncle, who told her about the newspaper, and her sister, who told her about the leaves, and went up to change her shoes.

On the stairs she stopped, and stood for a moment with one hand on the rail, as if she had heard her name. Then she went on.

Her room faced west, and at that hour it was full of a level, yellowish light that made the furniture look older than it was. She sat on the edge of the bed and took off her shoes, and then did not get up again for some time.

It is one of the small cruelties of a quiet house that there is nobody in it to interrupt a thought before it has gone too far.
